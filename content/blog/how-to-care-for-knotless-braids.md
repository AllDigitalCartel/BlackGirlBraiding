Knotless braids should give you six to eight comfortable weeks. Most people who get less than that aren’t unlucky. They’re doing one of three things that quietly shorten the life of the style.

Here is what we tell every client before they leave the chair.

## Week one: leave them alone

The install is the most fragile point. Give the braids a few days before you put them up, wash them or sleep without protection. The roots settle in that first week, and pulling at them before they do is what causes early frizz at the parting.

If anything feels genuinely tight, not snug but tight, call us. We would far rather loosen a section the next day than have you sit with a sore scalp for two months.

## Washing: every two to three weeks

The instinct is to avoid washing braids entirely. That’s a mistake. Product and sweat build up at the scalp, and a dirty scalp itches, flakes and eventually smells.

- Dilute a gentle shampoo with water in an applicator bottle.
- Apply it **to the scalp only**, in sections, and massage with the pads of your fingers.
- Rinse thoroughly. Squeeze water out down the length; never rub or scrunch.
- Dry properly. Damp roots are where mildew smell comes from, so sit under a dryer or give them real air time.

## Moisture: scalp first, length second

A light oil or a water-based leave-in on the scalp two or three times a week is enough. Heavy creams and thick butters along the braid length do very little except attract lint and dull the hair.

## Nights: wrap them, every time

This is the single habit that separates braids still looking good at week seven from braids looking tired at week four. A satin scarf or bonnet, or a satin pillowcase as a backup. Cotton pulls moisture out of the hair and roughs up the braid surface while you sleep.

## The three things that shorten the style fastest

1. **Re-tightening the front rows repeatedly.** It looks fresher for a day and costs you edge hair permanently. If the front has grown out, it’s time for a take-down, not a re-tighten.
2. **Wearing them past ten weeks.** Your natural hair keeps growing and shedding inside the braid. Beyond ten weeks the shed hair mats at the root and take-down gets far harder, and far more damaging.
3. **A tight daily updo.** Vary the style and keep it loose.

## When to take them out

Six to eight weeks is the window. Ten is the absolute limit. When you do take them down, take your time, work in sections and use plenty of conditioner. Rushing a take-down undoes everything the protective style was for.
